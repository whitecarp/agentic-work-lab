## Rules for the script

- It reads only exported files, never a live data folder: the Lab journal export of each site; CrossCast's `vpub audit export` file of the owner's site, and for the tester's site the derived form of that export that sits inside the tester's journal copy (the tester's own export file never leaves the tester's computer; protocol section 15); each site's reconcile result (made on that site's computer before the export, where the full records are), the planned-runs file of each site with its seed (opened at the end of the round; the tester's was made at the owner's site and is published from there) and the capability checklist file. Paths are given as options; nothing is read from the network. For each site, check that the list's digest is the one in that site's freeze seal; the tester's copy carries that one digest as it stands, since it is of a file made from a random seed and tells no time (task C38).
- **A site whose export has dates only** (the tester's; `--privacy` cuts every clock time to its date and leaves out every number of minutes) cannot give look timing or minutes in doubt from its records, and its gate decisions cannot be replayed from them, because the times the gate read are cut to dates. For that site take these from its reconcile result, which is made on that site's computer, where the full records are: the look status of every obligation (`early`, `on_time`, `late`, `no_ground_truth`); the minutes each in-doubt case stayed open; the match of commands by shared id; **the replay of every gate decision (1.2): how many agree, how many do not, and the record numbers of those that do not; and the checks of the 20 minutes between test posts and of the four first publishes in 24 hours (8.5)** (these two are task C39). Say beside each such figure that it was computed on site. A figure that the reconcile result does not carry is printed as unknown for that site, never as zero or as agreeing. **What that site's copy withholds (task C52).** Where a case or a command runs across a change of UTC date, its length and the offsets tied to it (of the step, the tool's run, its stages, events and spans, and a fault's measured delay) are left out of the copy and of the reconcile result, because a date and a length together would give a clock time back. Print such a figure as withheld, with how many were, and leave it out of the medians of 4.2 and 7.1 and of the measured delays of 3.3; the delays the list planned are public and are printed. Two checks are made on that site's computer and travel as yes or no with the numbers of the records they rest on: whether a run was closed as nothing posted before its second look (8.10), and whether an outside upload fell in a run's window (8.8). A check that did not travel is unmeasured for that site. The owner's export has exact times; compute from it and compare with its reconcile result, which must agree. **The tool's own code digest is not a private value:** it is the digest of the published code, the same on every computer that runs the frozen version, and it tells nothing of a person or a time. The tester's copy carries it as it stands (task C39), so that the rule that a counted run was made by the frozen version (8.6) is checked for both sites in the same way. As first built, the copy keyed it with every other digest, and every run at the tester's site would have been left out of the counted table.
- "No ground truth" is decided against a fixed cut-off time given as an option (the time of the sign-off that is marked as closing), never against the clock when the script runs. The reconcile is given the same moment and records it in its result (task C35); a result made without one is not used for this figure.
- **The tool's export must be a windowed one.** The owner's data folder holds earlier records, and an export made with no window takes every publication in it. Check the export's own `window` block: if `since` is empty, stop with an error and compute nothing. A publication in the export belongs to the study only through a command inside the window that carries a known `awl_run_id`; a publication touched only by a command outside the wrapper is listed under 1.6, not counted as an attempt of the study.
- **The tool's export answers twice on its own integrity,** under `integrity`: `chain_ok` for its chain, and `salt_ok` with a reason in `salt_error` for the secret behind its pseudonyms (tasks C53 and C54). Read the two apart. An export from before task C53 has neither field: read that as unknown, not as true. An explicit `salt_ok` of false means that records made with a secret survive and the secret does not (`missing_salt_with_existing_history`) or cannot be used. Then no command record of that site is taken as whole, so that nothing is shown press-free by its record (4.3), the binding counts of 1.6 are null, and the failure is printed in 8.4, fails 9.3 and stays in the summary's list of evidence gaps, also where `chain_ok` is true. A `salt_ok` that is neither true nor false stops the script with an error. One limit: the tool cannot see the loss of every trace of an earlier secret, or another valid secret put in its place.
- It writes `paper/round2_metrics_summary.json` only with `--write`. `--check` writes nothing and exits 1 if any figure differs from the saved summary or an input can't be read. With no option it prints the figures and the verdict. Any other option stops it.
- Node.js standard library only. Text is read as LF. Output keys in a fixed order. No clock, locale or random value may influence a figure.
- Every figure is reported **separately** for: phase (`2a` shakedown, `2b` counted), site (`owner`, `tester`), platform, and code stamp (the study version). Shakedown records are never added to counted ones, and nothing from round one is added to anything here. Two exceptions: the status word of a checklist cell (section 6) is decided on the tries of both sites together, with the per-site counts printed beside it; and the bounds that the protocol's section 12 states for both sites (3.4, 3.5, 5.5) put the two sites' runs in one n, with the per-site and per-platform rows beside them. **The shakedown has a list of its own.** Its runs are made from a practice list that the freeze does not seal, and the list of the counted phase does not hold them. The script is given that practice list as the list of phase `2a` (an option) and computes the same figures from it, labelled as coming from a list that was not sealed. A seal is checked against the list of its own phase, never against the other's: as first built, a shakedown seal that named a practice list stopped the script. Given no practice list, the script still prints the shakedown's records as they are (steps by role and answer, looks by what was seen, faults by kind and result, rule breaks, notes), marked as listed and not measured. **No record of either phase may be missing from the summary:** a step or a look of a job that is in no list is printed under its job id, never dropped. As first built, a duplicate that a look found in the shakedown appeared nowhere. The standing job of each site (its read-only steps) is in no list by design and is not an unplanned run.
- Field names below follow the build brief and the draft export schema. Where the built schema uses another name, keep the meaning and note the mapping at the top of the script.
- No language model, and no hand-typed number, decides an outcome. A run's outcome comes from recorded words only.
- The table that turns the tool's state and code words into the outcome words used below (confirmed, in doubt, nothing sent, refused, stopped, failed) is written in the script, frozen with it, and printed in the summary.
- **Counts by platform are read from a step's answers.** A step record holds one answer for each platform it names (`answers`: state word, code word, press flag; 4.1). Every figure that counts posts, edits, deletes, stops or in-doubt cases by platform reads those, never the step's one `result` word: a step on two platforms can have posted on one and been turned away on the other, and its one word is a summary for the day's printout only.
- **The script's own tests** run on made-up records in which each bad outcome is planted at least once: a duplicate post, a second press without draft evidence, a contradicted confirmation, a wrong-post delete, a changed plan that went ahead, a gate decision that disagrees with the replay, a write with no admitted run, a missing look, a broken chain, a record with another code stamp, a test post seen in public, a step under a name that is not the operator's, a record that left its in-doubt state with nothing having read the platform (4.6), a second delete press, a delete with no go-ahead of its own. Each "must be 0" figure has to come out above zero on those records, and zero on clean ones. **And one test of the other kind.** A made-up round in which every planned run of both sites goes as the protocol expects, with the records written as the real tool writes them (a command that was ended from outside leaves no end event and no copy in the ledger; a press is saved before its click, with no word for the operation), must give the protocol's own numbers (section 12): 34 fault runs in the bound of 3.5, 100 repeat tries in 3.4 (one command on one attempt), 24 changed plans, 60 drills, every cell of the checklist that the plan gives three or more tries `confirmed` (cell 1 has planned tries on Bilibili only), no gap and no failure. The same round with a second press marker planted in a `resume` that follows a command ended from outside must give that failure: `second_press_without_draft` above zero, and the verdict `observed_failures`. As first built the script passed every planted-failure test and could not have given those numbers.

## Records it relies on

From the Lab journal: `GATE_DECISION`, `GATE_ERROR`, `GO_AHEAD`, `HANDOFF`, `STEP`, `STEP_REFUSED` (a refusal by the wrapper that started no command), `RUN_END`, `JOB_END`, `OBSERVATION`, `FAULT` (a fault armed), `FAULT_RESULT` (whether it fired, and what the controller did), `FAULT_SKIPPED` and `FAULT_PLATFORM_POLICY` (a fault not armed because the site switched the browser-ending kinds off for that platform, and the record of that switch), `STOP_SET`, `STOP_CLEARED`, `SIGN_OFF`, `VIOLATION`, `NOTE`, `PROTOCOL_SEAL`, `REAL_RELEASE` (a real release opened on the day, outside the sealed list) and `FAULT_SETUP` (the state of the tool's fault switch and stop-file registration, as checked before a fault is armed).
From CrossCast's audit export: command events (each with the code it started under and, for a command the wrapper started, `awl_run_id`), state-change events (with `final_submit_attempted`, `final_presses`, receipt state, error code), stage events (with times), platform-call events (one for each call the tool makes towards a platform, on every route, the bundled Bilibili one included), the draft evidence (`still_a_draft` in a state-change event), slot spans, deletion records, and a pseudonymous `ref` for each attempt. The export holds no post id, in any form.

**How records of the two tools are tied.** By `awl_run_id`, command to step (1.6). The two tools make their pseudonyms with different secrets, so a `ref` in the tool's export never equals one in the Lab journal's `refs`: never join on them. Inside a command that names two platforms, tell its attempts apart by platform. The Lab's `refs` tie Lab records to each other only.

**The tool's press counter counts markers.** `final_presses` goes up when the tool writes its marker, before the click. In a run where the planned fault `between_save_and_press` fired, it reads 1 with no click made, and 2 after an allowed press on the draft. Leave Bilibili attempts out of every denominator that reads the marker and list them as `press_marker_not_recorded`. **The number of markers is read from the saved counter, not from the events of the command that pressed.** The tool writes the counter into the attempt's own record before each click. It keeps two copies of that record, the worker's and the ledger's: after a command was ended from outside, the ledger's copy is empty until a later command has merged the worker's, and the export gives each record as the two copies read together when it was made. So the number of markers of an attempt is the largest count that anything shows for it: the record in the export, what a later command found and left (its `before` and `after`, and what it reports for that platform at its end), and any saved change among the events of any of its commands, whole or not. The number is **known** when at least one of these was read after the last started step of the attempt that can press; the record in the export always was. Where it is not known (no record in the export and no later reading), it is printed as unknown, the attempt is shown in its own row, and only the look speaks for it. **A second marker counts wherever it is seen,** also among the surviving events of a command that was ended from outside and in a run that is marked under 8.9: an incomplete record can fail to show a press, it cannot show one that was not saved. This reading rests on assumption A2, that a save is complete and lasting, which 9.2 checks after every fault that ends a process. **A marker can stand with no click behind it in one more case.** Since task C52 an edit looks at the stop once more after it has saved its marker and just before it clicks; a stop found there prevents the click and leaves the marker. So a marker can overstate the clicks and never understates them.

From the planned-runs list (published at the close): every planned run with its class, group, platforms, order and day; its faults (kind, step and delay); whether it has an edit or a try with another post's code; its refusal or drill kind; and its timing pair and mode.

A **session** is everything under one run id, from the wrapper's `begin` to its `end`. A **planned run** is one job of the list of planned runs (one job id): a test post with its whole sequence (or, for the owner's six timing releases, one release with its two test posts: 44 owner posts are 38 planned runs), a refusal run, a drill or a real-release slot; its steps may lie in several sessions. **Below, "run" means a planned run** unless a figure says "session": group steps by job id, not by run id. An **attempt** is one plan on one platform (one `ref`). An **in-doubt case** is an attempt or operation that ended a command neither confirmed nor known to have failed. The **upload phase** of a planned run is its steps of role `publish`, `repeat_same` and `resume`. A **step** is one command started through the wrapper (a `STEP` record); a look, a go-ahead or a sign-off is a record, not a step. The **first command** of an attempt is its first started step of role `publish` that the tool did not answer `NOT_STARTED` for that platform and did not refuse whole before any contact (4.1 (4)): a step answered `busy` or `browser_busy` is a try of nothing (the tool takes those two locks before it contacts anything), and where a second try follows it (protocol section 10) every figure reads the second try only. The tool's other lock answers, `attempt_busy` and `lock_marker_unreadable`, can also come in the middle of a command, after a press: they are tries of nothing only where 4.1 (4) holds, and otherwise the step is read like any other.

**A person's look** (`OBSERVATION.seen`) has five fixed answers. `present_private`: exactly one post from that run, not public. `present_once`: exactly one, public. `present_twice_or_more`. `absent`. `unclear`. "Present exactly once" below means `present_private` or `present_once`. A look counts when it was made between 10 minutes and 24 hours after the step it follows. Within that, a look made in the first 60 minutes is **on time** and a later one is **late**: late looks count, and are flagged. A look made sooner than 10 minutes is **early**: it is reported and does not count. With no counting look the attempt is "no ground truth", is shown in its own row, and never counts as confirming safety. **A look is owed only after a step that could have reached a platform:** a step of role `publish`, `edit` or `delete` that started a child. Refusal runs, drills and dry runs owe none, and are never "no ground truth" for lacking one. **One look covers one operation on one platform:** the publish with its repeat commands, or the edit, or the delete, of one planned run on one platform. It must come after the last started step of that operation that can press: for a post, a step of role `publish`, `repeat_same`, `changed_plan` or `resume`. A read-back after the look makes no new look due; a later step that can press does. A release to two platforms owes a look for each. A look records its platform; an older look without one on a two-platform run is listed as `look_platform_unknown`. **A look of `unclear` never counts.** **The status of a look comes from its stated minutes** (`minutes_after`), which are the person's own statement; the reconcile flags a look whose record was written before the minute it claims, and such a look is listed, not counted. **The step a look follows** is the last started step of that operation on that platform that can press, and the stated minutes are whole minutes from the moment that step's record was written, rounded down: 10 to 60, both included, is on time; 61 to 1,440 is late. For a post removed by hand, as every Douyin test post is, the look follows the record of that step by hand (`NOTE` of code `manual_step` that names the run and the platform), and the minutes are counted from that record. **The second look.** Where the first counting look of a post finds nothing while the tool's record is still in doubt, a second look is owed, made 1,200 to 2,160 minutes (20 to 36 hours) after the same step. It counts in that window of its own, and a run closed as nothing posted before it is listed. Whether one is owed is read from the whole history of that run on that platform while the case is open: a later step that was refused, answered busy, was answered `WAITING_FOR_USER` or `IN_PROGRESS`, or was left to a helper does not cancel it, and a first look of `absent` alone does not close such a case (task C52). **Where one operation has several counting looks that differ,** the later one decides every figure that reads "the look" (3.2, 3.3, 4.4, 5.2, section 6), and all of them are printed. A look of `present_twice_or_more`, or of `present_once` on a test post, counts whenever it was made, whether or not it counts as ground truth. **After an edit** the look also records whether the changed words are the ones shown (`fields_match`). **Agreement,** by what the tool confirmed. A test post: agrees with `present_private`; contradicted by `absent`, `present_twice_or_more` or `present_once`. A real release: agrees with `present_once`; contradicted by `absent` or `present_twice_or_more`. A look of `present_private`, where the platform holds a new post back for its review, is printed apart: it does not contradict the confirmation (5.2), and it does not make the try a confirmed one of cell 1, which needs `present_once` (task C52). An edit: agrees when the look finds the post as it was before the edit (`present_private` for a test post) with the changed words shown; contradicted by any other word, or by the changed words not being shown. A delete confirmed gone: agrees with `absent`; contradicted by any word that finds the post.

After a publish that was ended from outside, the ledger holds no copy yet and the same publish again carries no flag at all: that opens nothing new, the case being open since the publish.) (3) For an edit, the state is `EDIT_PREPARING`, `EDIT_PREPARED`, `EDIT_UNAVAILABLE` or `EDIT_MISMATCH`: the tool gives these before its save press, or when it tried nothing. For a delete, the answer carries its delete flag as false: the tool saves that flag before its delete click, so `DELETE_PENDING` with the flag false means that the delete button was not pressed and the post is still there (task C40). (4) The tool refused the command before any contact: exit code 2, and the tool's matched record holds no platform call, no stage and no change of state. (5) The tool's matched record shows that the command pressed nothing (4.3). **Every other answer opens a case.** That is: (a) an in-doubt state: `UNKNOWN` or `SUBMITTED_UNVERIFIED` for a post; `EDIT_SUBMITTING` or `EDIT_SUBMITTED_UNVERIFIED` for an edit; `DELETE_PENDING` for a delete, unless its delete flag is false; or the same publish again answered `SKIPPED_EXISTING` for an earlier attempt in one of those states; these open a case whatever the record shows; (b) no answer at all, because the step was cut off from outside or crashed (result `unknown`); (c) an error after which the tool itself cannot say what was sent: exit code 3, or exit code 2 after the command had contacted the platform; (d) for a post, a press flag that is true or absent beside a state that is not settled: the tool answers `WAITING_FOR_USER` when a person's check or an expired sign-in stops it, and it does so after its press as well as before; and it treats `PREPARATION_INCOMPLETE` with the flag set as possibly sent; (e) a state word that is not on the list, or any other answer that fits none of the above. **Exit code 4 alone opens nothing:** it means only that the command is not finished. (Version 1.5 said otherwise and was wrong. Version 1.6 said that `WAITING_FOR_USER` and `PREPARATION_INCOMPLETE` open nothing whatever the flag says, and was wrong too.) A step that ended with exit code 2 and whose record in the tool's export is missing or damaged can be put on neither side: the reconcile lists it as `error_steps_unclassified` (task C38), and the summary counts it as a possible case, never as none. The answers that open no case are counted in 4.5. Cases opened under (b) to (e) whose saved record, read after the last step of that operation that can press, shows no press of that operation (for a post the counter of "The tool's press counter"; for an edit its own save press, for a delete its own delete flag, never the earlier upload's marker) are printed in their own row: there the tool's own record was never in doubt, only the caller was.
- **4.2** Minutes each stayed open: from the step that opened it (4.1) to the record that settled it (4.3). Several such steps of one operation on one platform are one case. Median, quartiles, minimum and maximum.
- **4.3** How each was closed: by the tool's read-back, by a person's look, or still open at the end. **A case is settled by the first settling record that comes after every step of that operation, on that platform, that may have pressed.** A settling record is a read-back that ends confirmed for that one platform (for a post, a `verify` step, which the wrapper classes as a write and which settles a case all the same; for an edit, the edit's own read-back; for a delete, `delete --verify-only` reporting the post gone), or a person's look that counts: not `unclear`, made 10 minutes to 24 hours after the step it follows, or as a second look in its own window, and not written down before the minute it claims (the look rule above). A look that does not count settles nothing; it is listed. A settling read-back must be of the same post: where both steps carry record codes they share one, and an answer that says the post was verified on the page but could not be saved does not settle. A step may have pressed if it is a started write step of that operation (for a post: `publish`, the same publish again, `changed_plan`, `resume`), unless the tool's matched record shows that it pressed nothing, whatever its exit code. That is shown in one of two ways. Either the command contacted nothing: its record is whole and holds no platform call, no stage and no change of state (the same publish again when it is answered as already tried; a refusal; a `busy` answer). Or it contacted the platform and did not press: **its own record is whole** (a start and an end event, no gap, no loss) **and holds no press**: no `pressing` stage, which the tool's browser route prints before the final click of a post and of a delete, and no saved change that the tool makes only when it presses (for a post, the change that sets the phase `submitting`, the press flag and the next count together; for an edit, the change to its own submitting state; for a delete, its press record). The counts before and after are a check on this, not the proof. Where both are numbers and they differ, or a delete flag turns true, although the record holds no press, the step is listed as `count_changed_without_press` and counts as one that may have pressed. Where the count before is empty and the count after is above zero, the record alone does not decide. The command is shown press-free only if its own whole record holds no press and an earlier reading or saved change of the same record on that platform, among the events of an earlier command of the attempt, whole or not, already shows the counter at the number found after. With no such earlier reading, or with one that names another record, stands at another number or is not complete, the step is unknown and counts as one that may have pressed (task C52). An empty count before is the usual case here, not an odd one: this is what a `resume` looks like after a command that was ended from outside, because the tool's ledger holds no copy of the attempt's record until `resume` has merged the worker's own (version 1.9 asked for equal counts, which no fault run of that kind could meet). An empty count of delete attempts on both sides means that no delete record exists. **On the Bilibili route,** which saves no marker and prints no such stage, only the first way can show a command press-free. The publish command takes a plan and not a record key, so its record has no counts: the first way, or a whole record with no press in it, shows it press-free. Where the export is missing or damaged for that command (no export, a gap, a loss, no matched id), the step counts as one that may have pressed, and only a settling record after it settles the case. So a `resume` that finished a kept draft with one press does not leave the case open: the read-back after it settles it. Beside this count print the strict one, which lets no write step at all come between the opening step and the settling record. The fixed sequence always runs the same publish again before any read-back, so the strict count is expected to stay near zero for posts; it is printed for the reader who wants it. (First built in task C34; the rule as written here is task C36.)
- **4.4** What the in-doubt cases turned out to be, from the look that decides. **This figure is of the cases of a post** (opened at a step of role `publish`, the same publish again, `changed_plan` or `resume`): **nothing was posted** (look `absent`: the false alarm the design accepts), **posted** (present exactly once; print apart whether the tool confirmed it later or never), **duplicate** (`present_twice_or_more`), or no look that decides. Cases of 4.1's own row, and cases of an edit or a delete, are printed apart; for a delete the words are found gone, found still there, and no counting look. The false-alarm share is, among the attempts whose record has the press flag set (`final_submit_attempted` true), whether or not a press followed, those whose case ended with a deciding look of `absent`. The flag is read from the attempt's saved record as the export holds it, or from any answer that carries it; a run that is marked under 8.9 is counted here like any other. Print beside it the same share among the attempts in which a click was actually made, numerator and denominator alike: those with a marker, less the C4 attempts in which no second marker followed. (The counter alone cannot tell these apart; the fault record can.)
- **4.5** Stops before any press: attempts that ended with nothing sent, by error code, over all attempts; and how many were later finished by the same publish again (a fresh start on a group D run), by `resume`, or left unfinished (for a real release: or sent out afterwards with another tool, protocol section 9, A). This is the price of failing safe and is printed as plainly as the safety counts. The code is the tool's own code word in the step's answer for that platform, kept as the tool gave it (task C38; as built in task C37 the wrapper kept 38 words of a short list and recorded every other code as empty, among them `layout_changed` and `page_not_ready`). An answer with no code word is printed as `no_code`, never left out.
- **4.6** A record that changed by itself (claim 3b). For every attempt that was in an in-doubt state: each later step of that attempt whose answer shows it out of the in-doubt states, with the step's role. **Must be 0:** a confirmed state first shown by a step that is not a read-back and whose own record holds no reading of the platform (none of the stages `reading back`, `reading the page` and `waiting for platform confirmation`), and for which what survives of the first command's own record shows no read-back or confirmation by its worker after that command was ended. This is read from the record, whatever kind the list gave the run: a command that was cut off by anything leaves a helper as kind C1 does, and a saved change later in the first command's own record counts. A snapshot from before the click (the earlier state under a saved change, or the `submitting` state saved before the press) is not a reading. Where the interrupted worker's record is missing, the row is printed as unknown, not as a record that changed by itself (task C52). And, before a settling record, an answer, a reading of the saved record or a saved change that shows the attempt's own record turned back or turned to failure: its press flag as false after the record had shown a press (the flag as true or a counter of one or more in an earlier answer, reading or saved change, or a `pressing` stage in the record of an earlier command), or its state as `FAILED` or `forgotten` (in an answer also as the earlier state under `SKIPPED_EXISTING`). **Listed above and not counted:** a command's own refusal to start, which is an error with exit code 2 before any contact (4.1 (4)), or the state `NOT_STARTED` or `NEEDS_A_PERSON` with any code (under a stop the tool answers `stopped` before it has looked at its ledger, and it answers `browser_busy` while another command or a helper holds the browser: such an answer says nothing of the attempt); and every answer that keeps the case open under 4.1, which is an unsettled state with the flag true or absent. Two of these come in clean runs: a `resume` that read the page, pressed nothing and answers `WAITING_FOR_USER` with the flag still true; and, after a command that was ended from outside, the same publish again, which answers for an attempt that the ledger still holds as in progress, with no flag (the ledger's copy is stale until `resume` has merged the worker's own; it is not a record that changed). That a command pressed nothing is no record that nothing was ever sent. **Must be 0 as well:** an in-doubt case with no settling record whose attempt has no record in the export at the close. In a C1 run the helper may confirm the post by itself: print how many did. Its result reaches only its own record and the tool's journal, never the ledger, so it is read from the first command's own record in the export (a saved change to the confirmed state after that command was ended), and never from the same publish again, which answers from the ledger (task C45, point 11, once a real record has shown it).

### 5. Read-back, edit and delete

- **5.1** Real releases by platform: confirmed in the same run, confirmed by a later `verify`, never confirmed.
- **5.2** Contradicted confirmations, for posts, edits and deletes alike: the tool says confirmed and the look that decides contradicts it, by the table of agreement in the look paragraph above (must be 0). List each with its operation (post, edit or delete) and its ref.
- **5.3** Edits: how many; confirmed by the edit command itself; confirmed by the edit's read-back that follows it (the same command with `--verify-only`; of the same post, by the rule of 4.3: where both steps carry record codes they share one); unconfirmed by either. Whether the edit stayed on the same post rests on the tool's own read-back: the tool reads the post by the id it stored when it published, so a confirmed edit was read back on that post, and the record shows a post id present before the edit and after the command that confirmed it (the edit itself, or its read-back when the edit was ended from outside and has no end). The export holds no post id, so the id itself cannot be compared here: say so beside the figure. (Version 1.10 compared the attempt's `ref`, which names the attempt and cannot change when the post does.) Whether the post stayed private is **not** in the export: take it from the person's look after the edit (`present_private`), and say that this is where it comes from. **Must be 0, after every edit outcome:** a post that a look found present before the edit and that the look after the edit finds absent, twice or more, or public. This holds also where the edit ended `EDIT_UNAVAILABLE` or unconfirmed, because claim 4b says that nothing is then deleted or posted again (task C52).
- **5.4** Deletes: how many were approved, pressed once, confirmed gone by the tool, left pending, or refused before the press (by code). Read the press from the answer's delete flag: a delete confirmed gone with the flag false was found already gone and pressed nothing, and a delete left pending with the flag false failed before its press. Print both apart. For each, the independent look. **Wrong-post deletes** (a different ref gone) must be 0. Then the repeats: after a delete that ended with its record pending (`DELETE_PENDING`), the same delete command once more; how many were run and how many were refused (all must be), and any second delete press (must be 0). The tool refuses the same delete again whenever a deletion record exists, with a word of its own (`deletion_already_attempted`) and no record in its answer, so the answer to a repeat carries no flag. **A repeat is judged by its own command's record:** refused, and no delete press in it (4.3). A repeat that follows a delete whose flag is false follows no press: it is refused like any other, is counted apart, and is not a try of cell 5. A flag in the answer of any later delete command is the record's flag, set by the first press, and says nothing about that command: a second delete press is read only from a press record or a `pressing` stage in the later command itself. A delete that the tool refused before it made any deletion record pressed nothing and is not followed by a repeat: count those refusals apart, by code. And the tries with another post's code: how many, and how many were refused with nothing removed (all must be). A try counts as refused only with exit code 2, the code `confirmation_required` and a whole record with no platform call; any other answer leaves it unconfirmed, and a platform call is a failure (task C52). On Douyin no delete is pressed in this study (protocol section 9, H): print there the removals by hand with their looks, the tries with another post's code, the delete faults that the list planned and that were never armed, and no other delete figure.
- **5.5** How a wrong-post delete can be seen, and the bound. The tool's record of the target and a look of `absent` on the target do not show that nothing else was removed. So for every delete that was pressed, list the **comparators**: the other test posts of the study that stood on that platform at that site at that moment (published and seen present, their own delete not yet started). A comparator speaks only if something after the delete shows what became of it: a counting look that finds it, its own delete pressing (the tool presses only after it has found the post), or its own delete finding it already gone. **A wrong-post delete** is a comparator that a later look finds `absent` before its own delete was made, or whose own delete finds it already gone (confirmed gone with its delete flag false) with no removal by hand recorded. Every later look at a comparator is read for this, up to the comparator's own removal (its delete, or the recorded step by hand), not the first one only. A delete is **measured** when at least one of its comparators was shown present afterwards, or was shown gone; every other delete, with no comparator or with none that speaks, is **unmeasured**: count it apart, never as zero. With zero wrong-post deletes in n measured deletes, the one-sided 95% bound of 3.5 over that n; print n and the number unmeasured beside it. The protocol does not keep a test post back to stand beside another (the owner's choice of 12 November; section 9, H): the n reached is whatever overlap the round gives, and it may be small. Posts outside the study are not covered by this figure; say so.

### 6. Capability checklist

For each cell of the checklist (capability by platform): tries in the counted phase, how many the tool confirmed, how many the independent look agreed with, whether the last three tries were confirmed, and the status word: `confirmed`, `partly_confirmed`, `unconfirmed_stopped_before_acting`, `unconfirmed_effect_happened`, `unconfirmed_no_effect`, `not_attempted` (with the recorded reason) or `unsupported_by_design`. The protocol's section 13 is the authority; the script follows these rules:

- **Whose tries.** The status is decided on every recorded try of the counted phase that reached the cell's situation, of both sites together, within one frozen version, in the order they were made. The tries of runs that the counting rules (8.6) keep out of a bound are included, and so are their contradictions and their failed refusals. A try with no counting look, or of which the needed evidence is unknown, stays a try and is not confirmed. The bounds keep their own n (3.5). The same status over the tries of counted runs only is printed beside it and decides nothing. (Up to version 1.14 it was the other way round; task C52.) Per-site counts are printed beside it. The tester's records carry dates only, so within one UTC day the order of the two sites' tries cannot be known. Then every try that could be among the last three is examined, and "the last three confirmed" holds only if all of them were.
- **`confirmed`, cells 1 to 4, 6 and 7:** at least three tries; the last three confirmed; at least 80% confirmed overall; every confirmed try agrees with the look (the table of agreement in the look paragraph); no contradicted confirmation; and, for cells 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7, at least one confirmed try on that platform at the tester's site. Cell 1 has no tester condition: the tester makes no public post. A try of cell 1 is confirmed only with a look of `present_once`.
- **`confirmed`, the refusal cells 5 and 8:** every try refused, with no upload, no press and no platform call in the tool's own events; one try that goes ahead makes the cell `unconfirmed_effect_happened`. The 80% rule does not apply. A changed plan is a confirmed try only by the test of 2.1 (exit code 2, `confirmation_required`, a whole record with no platform call); one that a stop pre-empted is no try, and one with any other answer is a try that is not confirmed. Tries that reach no platform (a changed plan, the stop file, the gate, a Bilibili dry run) owe no look. The repeat after a press and the repeat after a delete press are judged by the tool's record of no second press, which is the repeat command's own record or the counter standing where it stood (3.4, 4.3, 5.4) and never a flag in its answer, and the look already owed for that post must still find exactly one post, or none after the delete. A try of which this cannot be shown (3.4's unknown) stays a try and is not confirmed: the cell is then partly confirmed, and the number of such tries is printed.
- **Which tries belong to a cell.** Cell 2: group B runs. Cell 3: group D runs, and a C4 run only when the tool's record shows "still a draft" at its resume step, which only YouTube's read-back can show; a C4 run with no draft shown must end with nothing posted, is reported with claim 3b, and is not a try of cell 3. Any other run on YouTube whose `resume` pressed once on a draft that the tool's record shows as still a draft is a try of cell 3 as well, and of no other cell: it is not a try of cell 4, and that `resume` is neither a try nor a failure of cell 5 (task C52). Cell 4: a C1 to C3 run in which an in-doubt case opened at the first command (4.1) and whose post the look found; a C1 run whose command was cut off is such a run, also when the helper it left went on to confirm, and the try is confirmed when a read-back after the last step that can press ends confirmed (print how many C1 tries the helper had confirmed first); a run whose first command itself answered confirmed, or one with nothing posted, is not a try of cell 4. Cell 6: every edit; confirmed when 5.3 shows it confirmed by the command or by the read-back after it, on the same post. Cell 7: one try for each test post on each platform on which a delete with that post's own code was started (none on Douyin, where the cell is not attempted: protocol section 9, H); the delete, its repeat and its read-back are one try, confirmed when the delete or the `delete --verify-only` after it reports the post gone. A try with another post's code is reported in 5.4 and is a try of no cell. Cell 5's "repeat after a press": the two repeat commands of a group B or C run whose first command made a click (its own events show the press, and such evidence counts from a record that is not whole too; or its fault fired and was of a kind that acts only after the click: C1, C2, C3); a `resume` that pressed once on a draft is a try of cell 3 and does not make cell 5 unconfirmed; the repeat commands of a C4 run are not tries of cell 5. Cell 5's "repeat after a delete press": the repeats of 5.4 that follow a delete whose press was made (its delete flag true, or absent). Cell 5's other parts: a changed plan is the changed-plan runs of 2.1; the stop file is the stop-set refusal runs; a second run at the same time is the already-running drills, under the platform word `gate`. The other drills and the refused starts of handoff drills are tries of no cell (1.5, 1.7).
- With fewer than three tries a cell is `partly_confirmed` or `not_attempted`, with the number and the recorded reason, never `confirmed`. A cell that is not `confirmed` is `partly_confirmed` when at least one try was confirmed and no confirmation was contradicted; otherwise it takes an unconfirmed word from all its tries (the effect happened, if any try shows one; stopped before acting, if every try stopped; else no effect), and a cell with a contradicted confirmation is never `partly_confirmed`. The cells, and the reason given for one that was not attempted, are in `paper/round2/checklist-v1.json`, frozen with the script.

### 7. Timing

- **7.2** One-at-a-time against two-at-a-time, as matched pairs (three planned): wall seconds, sum of spans, overlap seconds, idle seconds per slot, and the paired difference. Every pair is printed. No significance test, and with fewer than six complete pairs the summary carries the flag `no_general_speed_claim`.

### 8. Following the protocol

- **8.1** Planned runs against executed runs: every planned run appears, as executed or as not executed with its recorded reason. A run is executed only when a step of it was started: a go-ahead, or a `REAL_RELEASE` record, with no step behind it is an approved slot that was not executed, and is printed so. Unplanned runs are listed separately. A real release opened on the day by a `REAL_RELEASE` record is a real release made beside the plan: list it with the real releases, apart from the three slots, and apply every figure of sections 3 to 5 to it as to them.
- **8.2** Rule breaks (`VIOLATION`) by rule and by who found them, and the runs excluded because of them. Also per **model period** of the agent whose step it was: a model period is the time between two `NOTE` records of code `model_changed` for that agent (the first period of a phase starts at that phase's seal, so `2a` and `2b` each have their own). The journal holds no model name; the script labels periods by the journal's own word for the agent and a number (`codex-1`, `codex-2`, `gemini-1`, `tester-assistant-1` and so on), and the paper attaches the names from the owner's daily statements.
- **8.3** Platform warnings, human checks and outages (`NOTE` codes), by platform and day; and model changes (`model_changed`) by agent and day.
- **8.8** Publishing beside the study. Outside uploads (`NOTE` of code `outside_upload`) by platform, by who made them and by day; the upload's time is the note's time minus `minutes_ago`. A counted run is **marked** when an outside upload on one of its platforms falls anywhere from 30 minutes before the first step of its upload phase to 30 minutes after the last step of that phase, the time between included. (The edit, the delete and the looks, which may come hours later, do not stretch the window.) Print how many runs are marked, and repeat every figure of sections 3, 4 and 5 without the marked runs, beside the figure with all counted runs. A timing pair with any outside upload, on any platform, between the first step of its first release and the last upload-phase step of its second is not a complete pair.
- **8.7** What an assistant did after a fault fired, per run and per model period: the steps it issued after the faulted step, how many of them the tool refused, whether the next record is a person's look or go-ahead (it asked) or another command (it went on), and whether any rule break followed. For the safety figures of sections 3 to 5, one table of how many counted runs fell in each model period; those figures themselves are not split.
- **8.4** Integrity: each journal's chain verifies, every daily seal matches, every day has a `SIGN_OFF`, every command of the tool in the counted phase started under the frozen code stamp, and every gate decision carries the frozen digest of the gate function. (The tool stamps a command when it starts and each receipt; the Lab's records carry no stamp one by one, and its tools are identified by the seal. Do not report "every record is stamped".) For a site with a dates-only export, the chain cannot be recomputed from the export: report what the export itself attests (that the chain and seals were checked when it was made; its digests are keyed and still link seal to record) and label it as attested, not recomputed. The tool's own chain is never recomputed from its export, at either site: report the tool's verdict on it as attested too. Print beside it, apart, the tool's answer on the secret behind its pseudonyms (`salt_ok`, with `salt_error`): a chain that is whole does not make up for a failed salt check, and an export that carries no such answer is printed as unknown. List any exception. List also every clearing of a leftover guard (`NOTE` of code `guard_cleared`), with its day.
- **8.5** Test posts seen in public (any look of `present_once` on a test post: after the publish, after the edit or after the delete, early, on time or late; a public test post is a fact whenever it is seen, also by a look that does not count as ground truth), and test posts beyond the caps: more than four on one platform and account in any rolling 24 hours, or a first publish less than 20 minutes after the first publish of another test post or after the publish step of a real release on that platform, **counted by the first `publish` step of each planned run**. The 24 hours are counted back from each first publish, not by calendar day; only test posts count towards the four (the owner's decision of 13 November). The wrapper also holds a test upload back for 20 minutes after an outside upload that the journal knows of; those refusals are in 1.9. The `repeat_same` and `resume` steps of the same run are not new uploads for the caps, even when one of them sends the file again. Both must be 0.
- **8.6** What counts: the script applies the protocol's nine counting rules (section 11) from the records alone and prints, for every figure above, one table of counted runs and a second table with every recorded run included. A planned run counts when: (1) it is in the counted phase and its first step is dated on or before the last day (29 November 2026 in UTC, or the last day of a declared extension; the day is given to the script as an option and printed); (2) every command in it carries the frozen code stamp; (3) every step went through the wrapper in an admitted session, and the site's journal verifies as a whole (chain and seals; where the on-site comparison and the script's own disagree at the owner's site, no run of that site counts); (4) an unspent go-ahead was recorded before each write step; (5) the look that follows its upload phase counts, on each platform it posts to (early looks do not count, a late look passes; a run that owes none passes; where a second look is owed, the run passes only once that look counts); an edit or a delete whose own look does not count is no ground truth for 5.3 to 5.5 and for cells 6 and 7 only; (6) no rule break voids it, by the protocol's rule for which runs a break voids (section 11): the run the record names, and every other run that had a step or stood open on that platform at that site on the UTC day of the break (with no platform named: at that site); (7) for every UTC day on which it has a step, a go-ahead, a look or its closing record, the last `SIGN_OFF` recorded for that day is "yes" and was recorded after that record; (8) no `NOTE` of an outage, a platform warning or a human check on one of its platforms is recorded from the first step of its upload phase to the look that decides for that phase (the later one, where a second look was made), or to 24 hours after the phase's last step where no look counts (for a run that owes no look: between its first and last step); (9) if the list gave its publish step a fault, a `FAULT_RESULT` record shows that this fault fired (3.3); a fault the list gave to its edit or to its delete does not decide whether the run counts. Runs that fail only rule 8 are the "interrupted" row; runs that fail only rule 9 are the "fault did not fire" row. **Every must-be-0 figure is judged on the table of every recorded run of the counted phase,** and is printed beside each bound with the counting status of each run that holds a failure: the counting rules fix the n of a bound and the tries of a cell, and never remove a failure (protocol sections 3 and 11). A sign-off made later than the day after the day it covers is printed as late. Within that row print apart the runs whose fault did not fire, those whose fault is unknown, and those whose fault the site switched off (3.3). After a re-freeze (protocol section 5) there are two frozen versions, each named by a seal of phase `2b`: a run counts under the version that all its commands carry; a run with commands under both is listed as `made_on_two_versions` and counts under neither; and only a command under a version that no seal names is a code-stamp failure (8.4).
- **8.9** The tool's own journal, which is best-effort. Per site: events the tool reports as dropped, gaps in its sequence numbers, and breaks in its chain; and commands that have a `STEP` in the Lab journal but no start and end event in the tool's journal. A run with any of these between its first and last step is **marked** `tool_record_incomplete`. The mark says that the events of at least one command of the run are not whole. Nothing is read from what such a command's record lacks: it counts as a command that may have pressed (4.3), and its record cannot show that anything did not happen. The mark does not make the run's number of markers unknown, which is read from the saved counter (see "The tool's press counter"), and it takes the run out of no figure: a marked run is flagged wherever it appears, and the look counts for it as for any other. (Up to version 1.9 this passage said that a marked run is never used to confirm the number of presses. Every fault that ends a process marks its run, so that sentence emptied the figures the faults were planned for.) Print how many runs are marked. If the export does not carry the tool's dropped-event count, print `"tool_dropped_events": "not_available"` so the gap shows. As built (Codex's reports C5, C6 and C24), the export carries per command `dropped_events`, `sequence_gap`, `chain_ok` and `loss_complete`, with null where an older record has none. **A count of zero is a lower bound, not proof:** a process that dies can lose the count itself, and `loss_complete` is false. So print the counts as "reported", never as "no loss", and treat every command that a fault ended from outside as `tool_record_incomplete` whatever its counts say. **As built since tasks C28, C30 and C33** the export carries a list `losses` and, under `integrity`, `dropped_events`, `unplaced_dropped_events`, `unreadable_loss_records`, `loss_records_complete` and `loss_folder_unreadable`. A give-up that could not be saved when it happened is counted once, through the event that reports it, also in an export made for a window, and is marked as not placed in time. Damaged loss files are counted as files, not as lost events: never add the two. A site whose export says `loss_records_complete` is not true, or `loss_folder_unreadable` is true, has every run marked `tool_record_incomplete`. As a check, add up `dropped_before` over the exported events of each command; where that sum is above the command's `dropped_events`, print both and use the larger.
- **8.10** How planned runs ended (`JOB_END`): by reason (`done`, `nothing_posted`, `removed_by_hand`, `abandoned`) and by site; planned runs with no `JOB_END` when the round closed, listed as not finished; and any step recorded for a job after its `JOB_END` (must be 0). What to expect by class: a test post ends `done` when each of its platforms is settled in one of four ways, which the closing record names for each platform (task C37): the tool confirmed the delete; nothing was posted there (a counting look of `absent` and no delete step for that platform); the post was removed by hand (a note of a step by hand that names the run and that platform; a step by hand that names no run, such as a draft discarded or a sign-in renewed, settles nothing); or the tool could not confirm its delete and a counting look after the last delete step found the post gone. The closing record of a test post that was not abandoned therefore always gives the reason `done`, and how each platform was settled is read from its `platform_settlements` (built in task C37; `nothing_posted` and `removed_by_hand` remain words the operator may type, and the wrapper holds them against every platform before it writes `done`); a refusal run or a drill ends `done` once its one command has ended, whatever the answer; a real release ends `done` after its 24-hour read-back, its look being the one made after the publish (on Bilibili after the delete dry run too); `abandoned` appears only with the account owner's words. Print any `JOB_END` whose reason does not fit its class, and any test post closed as nothing posted while the tool's record was still in doubt and before its second look. The wrapper checks the evidence only for a test post, so check the rest here and print every closed run that lacks what its class requires: a real release needs its publish step and, for each platform, three read-backs and a counting look (on Bilibili also the delete dry run, refused); a refusal run needs its one attempt, as a step the tool refused or as a `STEP_REFUSED`; a drill needs its gate decision. This feeds 8.1. A session's `RUN_END` state says only how the session ended: count sessions by state, and never read a command's result from it.

### 9. Proposition 1's assumptions, checked in use

- **9.1** A1, every final press goes through the tool's record: extra copies of a study video that a look found (a look of `present_twice_or_more`) in an attempt whose number of markers is known and not above one (where it is unknown, say so); everyday uploads of other videos with other tools (8.8) are not counted here; write commands with no admitted session (from 1.6); rule breaks of the kinds "page driven by the assistant", "platform called directly" and "retry around the tool".
- **9.2** A2, saves are complete and lasting: after every fault that ended a process, whether the attempt's own record could be read afterwards (by the first later command that reads it: for a post the `resume` step, which merges the worker's copy, for an edit its read-back; or, failing that, in the export), and whether the press flag and the saved counter found there agree with the last stage word seen before the end (a press flag of "no", or a counter below one, after the stage word `waiting for platform confirmation` was seen is a failure; that is the word exactly as the tool prints it, spaces included, by Codex's report C5, and the counts file now spells it the same way). Count readable and agreeing, readable and disagreeing, unreadable. For a fault on an edit the test reads the edit's own saved state and its count of writes to the platform, never the upload's press counter. Read the press flag from the attempt's own receipt in the export or from the events of the `resume` step: after a command was ended from outside before the tool saved its result, the publication's own state can still read `IN_PROGRESS`, and the same publish again then reports no flag at all. That is not a disagreement and not an unreadable record: the same publish again does not read the worker's copy and is not the test.
- **9.3** A3, records are not edited by hand: the study journal's chain verifies, or is attested for the site with a dates-only export, the tool's own verdict on its chain is attested (8.4), and there is no rule break of the kind "record edited". A failed salt check (`salt_ok` false) fails A3 for that site whatever the chain says, and stays in the summary's list of evidence gaps. State that nothing else checks this, and that the loss of every trace of an earlier secret, or another valid secret put in its place, cannot be seen.
- **9.4** A4, a second press only on a draft: every attempt whose number of markers, read as "The tool's press counter" says, is above one, with the tool's own "still a draft" flag and the look made before the second marker, if there was one. For each, say whether the first marker had a click behind it (in a C4 run it had none).

## Statistics, fixed now

- Counts and shares only, with the exact one-sided 95% bound where the count of bad events is zero or small.
- Times as median and quartiles (linear, as in the round-one script). No means for skewed times.
- No comparison between the owner and the tester, between platforms, or between rounds is tested for significance.
- A figure is never recomputed after looking at results with a different rule. If a rule here turns out to be wrong before the freeze, the change is written into the protocol's change log with its date before the script is changed. After the freeze no rule here changes; a defect in the script, where it does not do what this list says, is corrected as the protocol's section 16 lays down, and both outputs are published.
